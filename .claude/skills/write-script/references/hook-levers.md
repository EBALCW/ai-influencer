# Hook levers

Hooks are psychology levers, not formats. Pick the two or three levers that fit
this viewer and topic, draft at least two hooks for each, then keep the strongest
one, or as many as the creator wants to test, as openings A, B, C… Strong hooks
pull two or three levers at once.
Bracketed text is a shape to fill with true details, never a fact to reuse.

| Lever | Shapes | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity gap | "Nobody's talking about [X]." · "I keep seeing the same [N] mistakes." · "Wait until you see what [X] does." | A real gap, and a payoff the viewer can't predict |
| Status threat | "If you're still [doing X], you're already behind." · "Most [identity] get this wrong." | A real shift, and an identity sharp enough to recognize |
| Regret | "I wish I knew this [time] ago." · "I spent [time] doing this wrong." | A mistake the viewer is making right now |
| Loss aversion | "Let me save you the [N] hours I just lost." · "Stop paying for [X]." | A concrete time or money difference |
| Pattern interrupt | "Stop. Look at this." · a first frame that contradicts expectation | Used sparingly, with a payoff in the next breath |
| Specificity | "[Number] [unit] [result]." · "If you're [persona] and want [outcome] without [friction], try this." · "[3–7] [things] that [outcome]." | True, surprising numbers; distinct list items |
| Contrarian | "You might not agree with this, but [defensible take]." · "Here's the real truth about [X]." | A take that survives the comments |
| Confession | "I used to think [X]. [Event] changed that." · "[Time] ago I [hard situation]." | A real experience tied to the topic |
| Transformation | "[Old way] → [new way]." · "Used to take [long]. Now [short]." | A named bridge and a shown result |
| Direct address | "Are you a [persona] who [problem]?" · "Can I show you something?" | A persona the viewer recognizes in one second |
| Roleplay | A viewer question, then "Here's what I wouldn't do… here's what I'd do… watch." | A question the audience actually asks |
| Dichotomy | "[A] vs [B]: not even close." | Two real options the viewer has a stake in |
| Urgency | "It's gone after [date]." · "Do this before [event]." | A real deadline |
| FOMO | "[N] people already [did X]." · "Everyone's doing [X]. The smart ones do [Y]." | A real number, deadline or knowledge gap |
| Reverse psychology | "Don't install this." · "Stop using [tool] like this." | A payoff for the forbidden move |
| Villain / alarm | "[Authority] just flagged [problem]." · "The most useless feature of [app]." | A real villain, not a strawman |
| Too good to be free | "…and it's completely free." · "It's like hiring a [role] for $0." | A free thing that actually delivers |
| Borrowed authority | "[Company] built [X]." · "Someone at [organization] says [claim]." | A checkable attribution |
| Discovery flash | "Someone just built [X]." · "[Company] just shipped [X]." | Recency |

For text over footage, relatable openers work: "POV: [situation]." · "The
biggest lesson I've learned about [X] is that it's not [Y]." · a quoted question
with a funny answer.

## The stakes clause

When a hook states an interesting fact with no named beneficiary, add a clause of
about seven words naming who should care: "If you're paying for three AI
subscriptions, this kills two of them." It binds the right viewer and lets the
wrong one leave early, which helps the platform find the right audience.

## Keep the loop open

1. Name the one thing the viewer wants to know.
2. Don't say it in the hook. Reference it sideways or open a contradiction.
3. Build tension through the body by adding information, not by withholding.
4. Close the loop at the payoff.

These openers are closed: "Here's [N] things about [X]", "This is how to [X]",
"Let me explain [X]". Keep the number or frame and withhold the content instead:
"5 things that quietly broke my workflow" leaves the viewer asking how. A loop
that never closes gets called out in the comments.

## Re-hooks

"And the crazy part is…" · "It gets worse." · "But here's what's insane." ·
"That's not even the best part." · "One catch." · "Watch."

## News and launches

When being early is the edge, use this shape: a flat statement of what shipped
and a stakes clause (0–3 s), a fast walk through concrete features, a pivot near
the midpoint ("the best part isn't what you think"), the real payload, then the
implication or CTA. Real excitement is welcome when a concrete capability backs it.

## Quick check

Rate the finalists ✅, 🟡 or ❌ on curiosity, stakes, specificity, visual proof
and fit with the creator's voice. If every hook scores ✅ everywhere, rate again:
most have one or two weak dimensions. The idea matters more than the written hook,
the written hook more than the spoken delivery, and a visual gimmick least. A weak
topic can't be rescued by a strong line; go back to the viewer or the angle.
