# 3D Map Explainer

> Real terrain and satellite imagery under a low, warm sun, flown by a camera that dives from the globe into one place, slices the ground open, flies along the routes that matter and pulls back out to show where everything goes. Routes glow amber on the land and sea, numbers stand on the map as stacked pillars, and thin condensed capitals label it like a chart. A 9:16 reel, or a 16:9 video for YouTube.
> References (grammar only): long-form geopolitics and logistics explainers on YouTube (one question per chapter, the zoom from planet to place, a big number held on screen); relief cartography and shaded-relief atlases (low sun, vertical exaggeration, ink-like labels); engineering cross-sections (a cut through the ground, scales stated). Never copy their channel branding, map styles, typefaces or music, and never name them in the video.

## Formats

### Reel (1080×1920)

- **Layout.** Turn the camera's heading so the subject's long axis (a strait, a river, a route) runs top to bottom; the map bleeds to all four edges.

| Zone | Position | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Map | full bleed | the subject inside x 72–900, y 380–1150 |
| Kicker and number | x 72, y 300–440 | 24 px amber kicker, value at 72–96 px |
| Place labels | inside the safe box | at least 26 px; labels that drift outside it fade out over 40 px |
| Subtitle shelf | gradient from y 1000 to 1260 | one caption line centred on x 540, ≤ 720 px wide, baseline y 1200, 44 px |
| Data credit | x 72, y 1290, 16 px | decoration, always on |

  Comparisons stack (pillars one above the other, or side by side only if both fit inside x 72–900).
- **The first 3 seconds.** Frame 1 is the globe already close, the place pinned with pulsing rings and the camera moving. The dive starts by 1 s; by 2.5 s the camera is over the place; on the downbeat by 3 s the route ignites and the question is captioned.
- **Length and pace.** 25–45 s at 96 BPM (a bar is 2.5 s): dive, one measure or cut-away, one number, pull back. One camera move per bar; hold the number for two bars.
- **Captions.** The shelf of section 5, at 44 px.
- **Sound.** `zoom-swish` on the dive and the pull-back, `ui-pop` for pins and labels, `tick` for each measure tick, `sub-impact` when the ground is cut open or the routes ignite, `ratchet` under a pillar growing slab by slab, `shimmer` as the atmosphere passes. The pulse, bowed strings, chimes and harp from a free sample library.

### YouTube (1920×1080)

This is the style's home format: a 5–8 minute explainer watched on a TV, built from one continuous world at every scale.

- **Layout.** Section 2 as written. Captions sit on the shelf with their baseline no lower than y 930; numbers and headlines stay above y 950 and inside 96 px of the sides. The data credit line runs along the bottom right at all times (it is an attribution, not a must-read) and is repeated in the description.
- **Length and structure.** 5–8 min in 5–7 chapters of 40–90 s, built as a **zoom ladder**: each chapter lives at one scale and asks one question.

| Chapter | Scale | Question it answers | Signature shot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | globe → region | where is it, and why should I care? | the dive |
| 2 | the place | what makes it special? | the measure |
| 3 | under the surface | what is it physically like? | the cut-away |
| 4 | along the flow | what moves through it? | the lane flight |
| 5 | the numbers | how much? | pillars on the map |
| 6 | the alternatives | what if it closed? | routes drawing around it |
| 7 | back to the globe | who depends on it? | the pull-back |

  Each chapter opens on a downbeat with a **chapter block** written in the sky or over open water: a 17 px amber kicker (the chapter's number and scale), a 54 px title, a north arrow, held two bars while the camera keeps moving. Put the chapter times in the description.
- **Re-hooks every 30–60 s.** Alternate "where" beats (a change of scale: dive, crane, pull-back) with "how much" beats (a number grows, a measure lands, a route ignites). Plant a "what if" in chapter 2 and pay it in chapter 6. The longest a shot holds without a new event is one bar and a half (≈ 4 s); the longest a chapter stays at one scale is 90 s.
- **The first 10 seconds.** The globe with the place pinned on frame 1, rings pulsing, the camera already pushing. The place name and its coordinates write on beside the pin by 2.5 s; the question ("why does so much depend on this one place?") by 5 s; the dive and the promise ("by the end you'll know what happens if it shuts") by 10 s. The channel sting, if any, after that.
- **Breathing room.** Land each camera move before the number is said, finish drawing a route before explaining it, hold every pillar until it can be read twice.
- **The last 20 seconds.** The pull-back to the globe, framed off-centre (a 330 px view offset) so the right third is empty sky for the end screen; routes keep flowing; nothing must be read at the edges.
- **Captions.** The subtitle shelf is part of the look and is burned in; export the same lines as `.srt`.
- **Thumbnail.** The low oblique over the place with the glowing route and the measure or the one big number in Barlow Condensed 600, up to three words (a distance, a share, or "ONE WAY IN"), the land's warm relief filling the lower two thirds.

### Good for

News and geopolitics, energy, trade and shipping, rivers, borders and terrain, infrastructure and megaprojects, history of a place, natural hazards.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

1. **One continuous world.** The globe and the map are the same place at different scales, joined by aligned dives; the viewer never loses where they are.
2. **Real ground.** Elevation and satellite imagery from open data, shaded by a low sun with baked soft shadows and stated vertical exaggeration.
3. **Light marks the story.** Only what the video is about glows: routes, lanes, flows, the pin. Everything else is ground.
4. **Data stands on the place.** Numbers become physical (stacked slabs, a measured line, a cut section) at the location they describe, and every scale is printed.
5. **Chart lettering.** Tracked condensed capitals, ink-dark on bright land and pale on the sea, appearing only when needed.

Not **Radar Sweep Documentary** (a black world revealed by a beam). Not a flat infographic map: no flat-filled outlines, no choropleth over the imagery. Not a satellite-footage montage: the camera is a designed path through one world. Maps follow [directing](../../../references/directing.md#maps): no borders unless the video is about them, regional views over world maps, disputed areas kept out of frame.

## 2. Frame and layout (1920×1080)

| Zone | Position | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Map or globe | full bleed | field of view 28°; the subject in the middle third |
| Kicker + headline | x 96, y 118 / 162 | kicker 17 px amber, headline 40 px, a 14 px note under it (scales, sources) |
| Country / land labels | on the land | 30 px, weight 600, tracked 9 px; smaller ones 20 px |
| Sea labels | on the water | 21 px, weight 500, tracked 7 px |
| Islands, points | beside a dot | 15 px, weight 600, tracked 3 px |
| Pin | on the place | a 4 px amber dot with three rings expanding 6 → 40 px; a 1.2 px leader to the title block |
| Title block | 90 px right of and 170 px below the pin | coordinates 17 px amber, title 58 px tracked 7 |
| Big number | next to its pillar or in open sky | 70–96 px, weight 600, with an 18 px unit and a 14 px source |
| Subtitle shelf | gradient over the bottom 250 px | 3 px amber bar at x 74; caption at x 96, baseline y 930 |
| Data credit | right-aligned to x 1880, y 1054 | 11.5 px tracked capitals at 55 % |

Labels fade out within 40 px of the frame edge or the shelf, so nothing is cut. Nothing sits on a panel.

## 3. Materials and rendering

- **Terrain.** A height mesh from elevation tiles in a projected plane measured in kilometres, land exaggerated about ×3 (×3.2 suits coastal relief), sea floor from bathymetry. A wider, coarser mesh behind it fills the horizon.
- **Imagery.** A satellite mosaic graded before use: land desaturated to about 0.82 and warmed (×1.06 red, ×0.88 blue); water replaced by depth-shaded navy, the imagery's texture kept only in the teal shallows; a 2 px soft coastline.
- **Light.** One low warm sun (`#FFE2B8`, about 20° high) and a sky/ground hemisphere fill; terrain shadows baked into a texture by ray-marching toward the sun, the penumbra widening with distance, so nothing shimmers in flight. Water is smoother than land and catches a sheen.
- **Atmosphere.** Haze `#5F6872` closing in from just beyond the target to about three times the camera distance; ACES tone mapping at exposure 1.25.
- **Ribbons** for everything that flows: a strip as wide as the larger of a world width and a minimum pixel width, Gaussian across, revealed along its length with a bright head, pulses travelling along it, dashes for edges, wakes behind glyphs. Additive, not tone-mapped.
- **Glyphs**: ships and vehicles as flat silhouettes that keep about the same screen size at any height.
- **Section wall**: a vertical surface along a cut line drawing rock (warm browns), water (teal at the top, navy at depth, a faint line every 25 m) and the sea floor as a warm line, with its own, much larger exaggeration for water depth (×100–×200), always printed beside it.
- **Globe**: a world mosaic with the regional imagery blended in so the dive is seamless, a thin rim atmosphere (blue at night, warm by day), fixed stars.
- **Post**: physical depth of field with aperture proportional to camera distance (strongest in the low shots, a miniature feel), bloom (0.55, radius 0.55, threshold 0.95), a vignette darkening the corners by 42 % with a slight warm cast and saturation 0.92, soft contrast, 2× supersampling.

## 4. Colour logic

- **The ground is the imagery**, graded warm and quiet; it is never recoloured by data.
- **The sea is depth**: navy where deep, teal in the shallows.
- **Amber is the story**: the routes, lanes and flows the video is about, the pin, kickers. Gold for the number that is the point.
- **Teal is the comparison**: alternatives, capacity, the second estimate; a lighter teal for an uncertain range.
- **Ice is geometry**: dashed lane edges, measured lines, boundaries when the story needs them.
- **Labels take the ground's ink**: dark on bright land, pale on dark, sea-blue on water.
- **Never**: flat-coloured countries, rainbow data scales, red alarm colours (tension comes from camera and sound).

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|

| The number | `#FFD08A` |
| Comparison / its range / its number | `#2FA3B0` / `#8FD6DE` / `#9FE3EA` |

## 5. Type and captions

- **Barlow Condensed** (OFL), weights 400, 500 and 600, in capitals with tracking (3–9 px), for every label, number and title. Numbers in 600; units and sources smaller beside them.
- **Hierarchy (16:9)**: 96 px hero share, 70–76 px pillar numbers, 58 px place title, 54 px chapter title, 40 px headline, 34 px measured value, 30 px country, 20–21 px seas and small countries, 15–18 px kickers, islands and units, 11.5–14 px credits and notes.
- **Every exaggeration and approximation is printed** next to what it affects: "land ×3.2", "water depth ×150", "routes schematic".
- **Captions** sit on the subtitle shelf (a bottom gradient to 55 % of `#060A0F`) with a 3 px amber bar on their left: one line of Barlow Condensed 500 at 36 px in 16:9 (44 px in a reel), `#F6EEE0`, left-aligned like a map legend. They fade in 0.2 s, out 0.2 s. One language only.
- **Hold** every caption at least max(1.8 s, the spoken line + 0.6 s).

## 6. Motion

- **24 fps**, continuous; a 96 BPM grid with a 2.5 s bar. Cuts, ignitions and chapter blocks land on downbeats.
- **Camera keys in log distance**: distance interpolates logarithmically, so a dive from thousands of kilometres to tens feels even, with pitch and heading eased alongside.
- **Things draw, they don't pop**: routes reveal over 1–2.6 s behind a bright head; measures grow from the middle out (ease-in-out, 0.8 s), ticks after; labels fade in 0.6 s, out 0.5 s.
- **Pillars grow slab by slab** on an ease-in-out, one slab per eighth-beat, each slab capped by a bright ring.
- **Traffic** moves at a constant, believable speed, wakes trailing.
- **Never moves**: the ground, the sun (shadows are baked), the stars, the credit line.

## 7. Camera

| Move | Expresses | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| Globe push (altitude 26,000 → 4,000 km) | the place in the world | openings, chapter 1 |
| The dive (aligned crossfade into the map, pitch 79° → 45°, 1,200 → 230 km) | arriving | entering the region |
| Slow oblique drift (1–2° of heading per second) | looking around a place | naming, measuring |
| Low oblique, pitch 4–8°, strong depth of field | standing at the scale of the place | cut-aways, pillars, comparisons |
| Lane flight (25–30 km behind, pitch 13–15°, along the route's heading) | travelling with the flow | traffic, distances, rulers |
| Crane up and out (300 → 2,400 km) | the bigger picture | alternatives, regional links |
| Pull-back to the globe, off-centre | consequences | endings, the end screen |

Allowed transitions: the **dive** and the **rise** (map and globe rendered from the same computed camera, crossfaded over 0.8 s with a warm atmosphere flash peaking at 22 %); a **hard cut on a downbeat** between two continuous moves (one or two per chapter); the **cut-away** opening the ground. Forbidden: dissolves between unrelated places, flat 2D wipes, zoom blur, fast globe spins, any move that loses the viewer's sense of where north is.

## 8. Sound palette

- **Ambience by scale**: a low hush on the globe, a rush for the dive, light wind over land, surf at low altitude, a muffled undersea hush during a cut-away.
- **Foley tied to marks**: soft high clicks for each pin or terminal (panned to its position); a run of seven rising clicks for a measure, then a double tick; a 52 Hz thump with a short buzz as routes ignite; a long low tearing sweep and a 40 Hz thump when the ground is cut; a whoosh on hard cuts, cranes and orbits; a soft hiss of flow along pipelines; a low pass-by for each ship.
- **Score**: a minor-key 96 BPM pulse (plucked bass on the beat, cello spiccato eighths, a muted frame drum each bar); pentatonic hand chimes for pins; a climbing glockenspiel at the ignition; a low pedal under the cut-away; one rising plucked note per slab with a horn swell; a bowed line following each drawn route; near-silence while a comparison is read; harp and a chime per route on the globe.
- **Silence**: one bar of near-silence before the biggest number.
- **Mix**: the music's 300 Hz–4 kHz band cut by 4 dB, then ducked under every line; voice about 10 dB over the bed. Master −14 LUFS, true peak −1 dB.

## 9. Native moves

1. **The dive.** From the pinned globe into the map without a seam. Build: convert the map camera to a globe camera for the crossfade; blend the regional imagery into the globe. Fits content like: any place-based story; a chapter change of scale; "where is this?".
2. **Ignition on the downbeat.** Lanes or routes draw from one end with a bright head, then pulses flow along them. Fits content like: shipping lanes; migration routes; supply chains.
3. **The cut-away.** A vertical plane removes the ground on the camera's side, revealing a section with depth lines and its exaggeration printed. Build: one plane test in every shader (`discard` on the camera side) and a wall mesh along the line. Fits content like: how deep the water is; tunnels and aquifers; why a valley floods.
4. **The measure.** A pale line grows from the middle out between two named points, ticks land, the distance appears in two units. Fits content like: chokepoints; the width of a river; distances between rivals.
5. **Pillars on the map.** A number stands where it happens, one slab per unit, with the comparison beside it on the same scale. Fits content like: flows per day; production by site; population by city.
6. **Flying the lane.** Low behind the traffic, with a ruler across the lanes and buffer. Fits content like: traffic separation; road and rail corridors; flight paths.
7. **Where it goes instead.** The camera cranes out and alternative routes draw across land between named end points, in teal. Fits content like: bypass pipelines; detours; "what if it closed".
8. **The pull-back.** Out to the globe, routes spreading to their destinations, the share written large in the empty sky. Fits content like: dependence; global reach; the ending.

## 10. Build

Follow [building](../../../references/building.md). For this style:

- three.js with two scenes: the map in projected kilometres (Web Mercator from elevation tiles, heights × the exaggeration) and the globe (radius 6,371 km). Render the active one, or both through a dive.
- Data, offline: elevation from open terrain tiles (SRTM/GEBCO-derived, public domain or attribution); imagery only under a licence that allows reuse (the 2016 Sentinel-2 cloudless mosaic is CC BY 4.0, later releases are CC BY-NC-SA and not usable); routes and lanes from OpenStreetMap (ODbL, credited); world geometry from Natural Earth. Log every URL, licence and date; tell the user before downloading.
- Prepare offline in Python: crop and grade the imagery, mask and depth-shade the water, write the height grid, bake the sun shadows.
- `MeshStandardMaterial` for terrain with the baked shadow multiplied into direct light (patch the lights chunk), roughness from the water mask, and the cut-away test injected with `onBeforeCompile`; custom ribbon shaders for every flow.
- Post: a depth-of-field pass (circle of confusion from `|1/focus − 1/z|`, a spiral gather), `UnrealBloomPass`, a grade pass, output; 2× supersampling.
- Camera paths as keyframes of log distance, pitch, heading and target; labels projected from their coordinates every frame onto a 2D canvas at 2×; every pin, measure, ignition, slab and cut also goes to the events list for the sound.

## 11. Pitfalls

- **Licences.** Imagery that looks free often isn't; check each source's terms and carry the credit on screen and in the description.
- **Borders and names.** A border drawn when the story is not about it, or a disputed area named as a country, turns a map into a statement. Follow the maps section of directing.
- **Hidden approximations.** Unlabelled vertical scales and schematic routes shown as exact mislead; print the scale, say "schematic", give every number a source and a year.
- **Labels in the wrong place.** Labels that sit under the shelf, cross the frame edge or overlap a glowing route; fade them and check every frame where the camera moves.
- **Shimmer and aliasing.** Real-time shadow maps flicker in flight; bake them. Thin lines need a minimum pixel width.
- **Lost orientation.** A heading swinging past 90° without reason, or a cut between places with no dive.

## 12. Variation space

The agent decides per video: the place and its scales, the exaggeration, the sun's direction and the time of day, which flows glow amber, the comparison in teal, the order of the zoom ladder, the chapter titles.

- **Three structures:** the zoom ladder (globe → place → under → along → numbers → alternatives → globe); a journey along one route from source to destination, one chapter per leg; a then-and-now of one place, the same camera path flown twice with different data.
- **Three openings:** the pinned globe with the camera already pushing; low over the place with the route igniting on the first downbeat; the cut-away already open on a surprising depth.
- **Three endings:** the pull-back to the globe with the share in the sky; the camera rising off the place as its routes keep flowing; a final measure across the same line as the opening, now carrying a different number.
