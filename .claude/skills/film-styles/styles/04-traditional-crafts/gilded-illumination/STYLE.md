# Gilded Illumination

> A painted manuscript world of flat mineral pigments, ink outlines and white-lead dots, where only the burnished gold leaf answers to light: a lamp, a sunrise or a sweeping band makes each leaf square flash as it passes, while the camera travels one continuous painting inside a patterned border. A 9:16 reel, or a 16:9 video for YouTube.
> References (grammar only): Gothic books of hours (flat pigment landscapes, stepped leaning rocks, scalloped clouds, patterned borders); Sienese gold-ground panel painting (tooled and punched gold, haloes as raised discs); medieval continuous narrative (the same figure painted several times along one path); the craft of water gilding (red bole under the leaf, squares laid in rows, burnished until it mirrors light). Never copy their characters, layouts, typefaces or music, and never name them in the video.

## Formats

### Reel (1080×1920)

- **Layout.** The painted world is tall (about 2700×4800 world units): sky and the light source in the top third, the path zig-zagging up through the middle, the starting place at the bottom. The patterned border bleeds at full frame width; its 60–70 px band sits outside the safe box, so everything inside x 72–900, y 269–1248 is picture. The hero and any plaque stay inside that box; the speech scroll caption sits across y 1110–1240.
- **The first 3 seconds.** Frame 1 is inside a single piece of gold (a star or an ornament) at about ×20 zoom, its leaf seams and crinkles catching light; it is already pulling back and turning by 0.5 s; by 2.5 s the painted world around it is clear, and what is at stake (missing, broken, threatened) is visible by 3 s.
- **Length and pace.** 30–45 s, five to seven shots. The journey along the path is the middle third; the turn is the hardest beat, followed by the slowest shot.
- **Captions.** The vellum speech scroll, centred on x 540, at most 720 px wide, one line of 34–36 px in italic.
- **Sound.** The pack's `shimmer` (gold catching light), `sparkle` (stars lit by a sweeping band), `clean-ding` (the touch that restores light), `sub-impact` (the moment of darkness), `metallic-riser` (into the turn). The medieval band, bells and foley come from a free sample library.

### YouTube (1920×1080)

- **Layout.** Section 2 as written. Raise the speech scroll so its lower edge sits at y 940; the border may run into the bottom 12 %, since it is ornament, never text.
- **Length and structure.** 3–8 min. **The video is one large painted world** (about 4800×2700 units per chapter region, regions joined along one path), and each chapter is a place on it: the camera arrives, the place's gold is lit, its scene is played in two or three shots, and the hero is painted into the place as a new figure in a gold aureole before the camera moves on. Chapter titles appear on a small gilded plaque that rises in the lower border: the title is written in red bole, gilded, then flashes. Every copy of the hero stays painted where it was, so the path becomes the chapter list.
- **Re-hooks every 30–60 s.** Night falls or lifts; light reaches a new piece of gold; a quatrefoil close-up on a face or object; a wipe by a near rock or a passing cloud bank; a band of light sweeping across the sky; a new copy of the hero painted in, layer by layer.
- **The first 10 seconds.** Strongest image first: inside the gold, pulling back to the full painted sky. The question or problem by 5 s; by 10 s the hero and the place they must reach are both in frame, which is the promise.
- **The last 20 s.** Pull back over the whole world as every copy of the hero glows, the border grows in from the four corners and the plaque arrives: picture-only edges for the end screen.
- **Captions.** The speech scroll is part of the painting, so it stays burned in; export the same lines as `.srt`.
- **Thumbnail.** The wide frame with the path of gilded aureoles climbing to the brightest gold, inside the border, with at most three words in blackletter on the plaque.

### Good for

Fables and picture-book stories, holiday and festival shorts, brand and origin stories, histories told as legend, journeys and pilgrimages, values and mission films.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

1. **Flat pigment, real gold.** Every surface is flat mineral colour with an ink outline, a darker inner rim and white-lead highlights, except gold, which is laid as leaf and behaves like metal.
2. **Only gold catches light.** Lamps, the sun and sweeping bands brighten gold leaf square by square; pigments never shine, they only darken at night.
3. **One painting.** Every shot is a part of the same world; the camera travels it; the border frames the whole.
4. **Continuous narrative.** The hero appears several times along the path, once for each moment of the story, each inside a gold aureole.
5. **Made by hand, in layers.** Figures are painted in visibly: gold first, colour next, ink and white lead last.

Not **Stained Glass** (no lead cames, no transmitted light; this is opaque paint on vellum). Not **Paper-cut Lightbox** (no shadows between layers, no depth stacking). Not a **storybook watercolour** (no washes or soft edges; every edge is an ink line).

## 3. Materials and rendering

- **Pigments** are flat fills with an inner rim band in the darker version of the same colour (8–18 units wide) and a 2.4-unit ink outline in iron-gall brown. Line width follows the camera so it never becomes a hairline: on screen about 4 × zoom^0.38 px.
- **White lead** is the highlight: thin lines along the lit edge, and dots laid as triads, pearl rings and pearl rows.
- **Ultramarine** carries a coarse granular texture (ground pigment reads as powder), and a 14 % multiply grain covers the whole picture.
- **Gold leaf** is laid in 100-unit squares in rows, each row offset; each square has its own tone and tilt with a soft burnish gradient along it; seams show as a dark line beside a bright ridge; red bole shows at one broken corner in four. Crinkles, pinholes and burnish scratches fade in only above about 2× zoom.
- **Tooling**: rings of punched dots, incised circles and diaper patterns of lozenges and rosettes on gold grounds.
- **The light pass**: a mask records every gold pixel; per pixel, the light from each source (radial for a lamp or the sun, a band for a raking sweep, rings spreading from a touch) is weighted by how the square's tilt faces it, the square is cupped slightly, and the leaf's own texture is brightened rather than replaced. Then a soft bloom from a quarter-size record, and four-point star flashes where light is strongest.
- **Night** multiplies the whole picture by a cool blue-grey and darkens the gold; a carried lamp adds a warm pool around itself under the gold's own light.
- **Vignette**: deep blue at night, warm brown by day, about 24–40 % at the corners.

## 4. Colour logic

- **Gold is the only yellow.** No large ochre or yellow paint anywhere; if it reads as yellow it is leaf and it can catch light.
- **Lapis owns the sky, vermilion owns the accents.** Roofs, garments and the border's warm segments are vermilion; skies and cloaks are lapis or ultramarine; land is verdigris greens.
- **Distance is paleness.** Far rocks in pale lilac, azure, rose and green tints; nearer ones in full pigment.
- **White lead is never a fill**, only highlight.
- **Darkness is a state, not a palette.** Night dims everything by multiply; the light returning is the story.

## 5. Type and captions

- **Faces** (all OFL): Grenze Gotisch for titles in blackletter; Uncial Antiqua for chapter numbers and short inscriptions; IM Fell English roman and italic for captions. For a script these lack, a serif such as Noto Serif.
- **The plaque**: a gilded frame, a vermilion field, the title in blackletter written first in red bole, then gilded, then flashed by a passing light; one subtitle line under it in Fell roman.
- **Speech scroll**: a vellum banderole with a slightly waving top and bottom edge, forked tails folded back at both ends, a 9-unit darker rim, an ink outline and a thin white-lead line along the top. It unrolls from the centre, stays open across gaps shorter than 0.9 s, and the text wipes in left to right over 0.55 s while the previous line fades out just before. Every scroll is the width of the film's widest line, so it never jumps. Text in IM Fell English italic, 32–36 px, ink brown. Hold each at least max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).
- **No floating type**: every word on screen sits on a scroll, a plaque or a painted inscription.

## 6. Motion

- **30 fps**, smooth. Nothing boils: this is a finished painting.
- **Figures walk** along the path by arc length with a small step and bob; the lamp rises from the side to above the head over about 0.6 s with a slight overshoot.
- **Painting in**: a figure arrives in three layers, each taking about a third of 0.9 s: the gold of its lamp and aureole, then its colours, then ink and white lead.
- **Light moves, paint doesn't.** Bands sweep at a steady speed; rings spread with ease-out; lamp pools follow their lamp.
- **The film clock**: write the shots on a story clock, then map the viewer's clock onto it with a monotone curve, so a walk can be quicker and the turn or the final hold slower without rebuilding any shot.
- **Easing**: cubic in-out for camera keys, monotone cubic tracks through several keys, cubic out for arrivals.
- **Never moves**: the border once grown, the painted landscape.

## 7. Camera

Camera `(x, y, zoom, rotation)` over the one world; zoom in log space; rotation only on the opening and on close-ups, under 0.05 rad.

| Move | What it expresses | What it can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Pull back from inside a piece of gold (×26 → ×0.5 over 5 s, unturning 0.3 rad) | the world opens from one precious thing | every opening |
| Track along the path with the hero (zoom 1–1.5) | the journey | the middle of any story |
| Quatrefoil close-up (zoom 2.5–3.5 inside a gold-ground window) | intimacy, a decision | faces, hands, the object that matters |
| Rise past the summit with a cloud wipe | ascent, effort | climbing, struggle, the last stretch |
| Wide hold with a band of light crossing it (2–3 s) | the change reaches everyone | turning points, dawns |
| Pull back to the whole world as the border grows (4–5 s) | the story as a whole | the ending |

Transitions belong to the painting: a near spire clump crossing the frame right to left, a bank of scalloped cloud passing down as the camera rises, or the quatrefoil window opening and closing. Forbidden: dissolves, shakes, whip pans.

## 9. Native moves

1. **Light finds the gold.** A carried lamp moves through the scene and every piece of gold flashes and chimes as it comes into reach, one at a time. Build: a radial light at the lamp; record each gold point's first lit frame for the chimes. Fits content like: a discovery spreading; a guide walking through a place; a product lighting a room.
2. **Continuous narrative.** The hero is painted again at each place they reached, in a gold aureole, so the path holds the whole story at once. Build: a list of copies with a paint-in time each; layer them gold → colour → ink. Fits content like: a journey; a founder's milestones; stages of a process.
3. **Opening inside the gold.** The first frame is one leaf square's crinkles and seams; the pull-back reveals it as a star among hundreds. Fits content like: one person among many; a small thing that matters; the start of any legend.
4. **The raking band.** A wide band of light crosses the picture and lights the stars and fruit in sequence, right to left. Fits content like: a dawn; news reaching everyone; a turning point.
5. **Light from a touch.** Rings of light spread from one contact point over a dark piece of gold (a crown, a disc, a door) until it blazes, its rays or ornaments re-lighting one by one. Fits content like: a gift given; ignition; a handshake that changes things.
6. **The quatrefoil window.** A four-lobed opening in a punched gold ground frames a close-up, then scales away to return to the world. Fits content like: a face at a decision; an object's detail; a remembered moment.
7. **Border and plaque.** At the end the border grows from the four corners and the plaque's title is written in bole, gilded and flashed. Fits content like: titles; a brand sign-off; a dedication.

## 10. Build

Follow [building](../../../references/building.md). For this style:

- Canvas 2D, every frame drawn from `t`: the world through the camera matrix into an offscreen frame, a gold mask drawn alongside it, then the light pass, bloom, flashes, vignette and scroll in screen space.
- Shared drawing helpers: `paint` (fill, rim, ink), `leaf` (squares, seams, bole, burnish, fine overlay by zoom), white-lead `triad`, `pearls` and `pearlLine`, `star8`, `tubePath` for paths and rivers, `spirePts` for stepped rocks.
- Textures are generated once and tile: the gold body, fine gold overlays, specks, pigment grain.
- Each shot is a function of story time returning camera, world state (night, which hero copies, lamp lift, border growth, plaque time) and its lights; `render(t)` maps film time to story time and picks the shot.
- Screen-space overlays (near rock, cloud bank, quatrefoil) also write an occlusion mask so gold behind them gets no light.
- Export events for each gold piece first lit, each star lit by the band, each paint-in, each wipe, the touch, the border and the plaque, for the foley and `cues.json`.

## 11. Pitfalls

- **Yellow paint that looks like gold** breaks the rule that only gold catches light.
- **Gold that is flat yellow.** Without squares, seams and per-square response it reads as a fill; keep the leaf structure at every zoom.
- **Hairline ink in wide shots.** Scale ink width with zoom.
- **Too much glow.** Bloom and flashes belong to the brightest gold only; the rest of the leaf should stay mottled metal.
- **Night too dark at phone size.** Keep the hero's colours readable under the night multiply; the lamp pool helps.
- **Cuts without cover.** Hide every jump in the path behind a rock or cloud wipe, or the illusion of one painting breaks.
- **The app's buttons.** In a reel, nothing that must be read below y 1248 or right of x 900; on YouTube nothing in the bottom 12 %.

## 12. Variation space

The agent decides per video: the world's geography, the light source and what darkness means, the hero and its emblem, which pigments dominate (keep lapis, vermilion and gold), the number of places on the path, and the band's instruments.

- **Three structures:** a quest along the path to one goal; a pilgrimage of stations, one lesson each; a year of feasts, the same landscape repainted by season with gold marking each feast.
- **Three openings:** inside a gold star pulling back; the hero's emblem glinting in a close-up quatrefoil; the border already drawn and the camera diving through it into the picture.
- **Three endings:** the whole world lit with every copy of the hero glowing and the border complete; a band of light leaving the picture as the plaque gilds; a quatrefoil close-up on the hero, then the window closing to gold.
