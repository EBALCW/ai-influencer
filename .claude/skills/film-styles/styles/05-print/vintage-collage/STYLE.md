# Vintage Engraving Collage

> Old engravings cut out with scissors and slapped onto a tabletop of foxed paper, among torn sheets of teal, ochre and mustard, halftone clippings, typewritten slips and ransom-note letters. Pieces jump on twos, land with a wobble and cast real shadows; the camera glides over the table and dives through holes in the paper. A 9:16 reel, or a 16:9 video for YouTube.
> References (grammar only): 19th-century wood and steel engraving (book plates, banknotes, stamps) for the swelling line; the engraving collages of the Surrealists, such as Max Ernst's collage novels, for recombining cut engravings at odd scales; 1970s cut-out animation, such as Terry Gilliam's, for paper that moves on twos and lands with a slap; ransom-note and zine lettering; music-hall brass and oom-pah marches for the score. Never copy their images, layouts, typefaces or tunes, and never name them in the video.

## Formats

| Hero cut-out | centred on x 540, y 720 | engraving with its white cut margin, rotated −2° to −4° |

- **The first 3 seconds.** Frame 1 already shows two torn colour papers mid-slide over the old paper; by 0.5 s the first lands with a slap and a wobble; by 1–2 s the hero engraving lands; by 3 s the ransom headline has pasted itself on letter by letter (one every 0.125 s) and the voice has asked the odd question about the object.
- **Length and pace.** 20–45 s. Something slaps, slides or is typed every 0.5–1.5 s; one through-the-hole dive or paper-seam whip every 6–10 s; one quiet beat where nothing new lands.
- **Captions.** A card slip typed at the bottom of the safe box, starting with the voice line.
- **Sound.** `hook-impact` on the first slap, `thock` on each later landing, `typing-tick` under typed slips, `clean-ding` as the carriage bell when a slip finishes, `zoom-swish` on whips and dives, `ratchet` under a press or roller. ### YouTube (1920×1080)

- **Layout.** Section 2 as written. The caption slip's bottom edge stays at y ≤ 936; headlines and tags stay inside x 96–1824 and y 54–936.
- **Length and structure.** 3–8 min in chapters of 45–90 s. Each chapter is one tabletop arrangement with its own hero engraving and lead colour paper; its card is the title pasted in ransom letters over a typed date slip. Move between chapters through the medium: dive through a hole in the last piece (an oval frame, a window, a keyhole), or whip the table sideways across a torn paper seam. Re-hook every 30–60 s with a physical event: scissors cutting, copies scattering, an engraved bar growing, a red stroke striking out a number.
- **The first 10 seconds.** The strongest image first: the hero engraving slapping onto torn colour paper by 1 s; the ransom headline pasted by 3 s; the question typed on a slip by 5 s; the promise (what the viewer will know by the end) by 10 s, while a tag slip points at the one detail that matters. Any title sting comes after.
- **Captions.** The typed slip is burned in; export the same lines as `.srt`.
- **Thumbnail.** The hero engraving large on two colour papers, pushed in until its lines read, one or two ransom-letter words beside it, one red tag on the detail.

### Good for

The history of an object, magazine-style explainers on ideas and society, archive and collecting stories, inventions and patents, money and prices over time, museum and heritage pieces.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

1. **Cut, not drawn.** Every image is a piece of paper: an engraving cut out with a rough white margin, a torn sheet of flat colour, a clipping, a slip. Nothing is painted on the table.
2. **Real paper physics.** Pieces have height: they cast two shadows (sharp and soft) that grow and soften as they lift, show a darker cut edge, and show their pale back when they flip.
3. **Paper moves on twos.** Pieces step at 12 fps and jitter slightly while moving; the camera glides smoothly at the full frame rate. The contrast is the look.
4. **The line is engraved.** Tone is made of parallel lines that swell where it is dark and thin where it is light, never of grey fills; even charts are engraved.
5. **Words are found objects.** Headlines are ransom-note letters on scraps of different papers; facts are typewritten slips; corrections are struck out in red ink.

Not **Copperplate Engraving** (one continuous engraved plate; here engravings are cut up, mixed with colour paper and type, and moved as objects). Not **Pop Art** (no bright process inks or dot walls; colour here is faded paper). Not a scrapbook template: edges tear, shadows follow height, pieces wobble on landing.

## 3. Materials and rendering

- **The table.** Cream paper with broad mottling (±3.5 %), hundreds of short dark fibres (6–26 px) and foxing (soft brown spots 10–60 px), baked once and tiled by mirroring.
- **Torn colour paper.** A shape whose edge is pushed in and out by fine noise only near the boundary, then sharpened; a thin white fibre fringe outside it (the torn core), 6 % mottling inside, a soft shadow 4–6 px down-right.
- **Engraving cut-outs.** A scanned engraving becomes ink (paper level → none, darkest 3 % → full); the subject is masked and a 4 px white margin with a slightly rough edge is dilated around it, as if cut with scissors. Optional hand-tinting lays 85 % of one paper colour under the lines.
- **Re-inked prints.** A two-colour archive print separates into plates by colour difference, sharpened at 2× and re-inked in the palette's red and blue, so plates can move separately.
- **Halftone clippings.** A photo re-screened as round dots on a 45° grid (≈ 7.5 px cells, 92 % ink) on newsprint `#E6E0D2`.
- **Engraved data.** Bars drawn as parallel lines whose width runs from 6 % to 92 % of a 7 px spacing as tone darkens, cross-hatched on the shadow side, over a faint ochre wash. A ratio too big to draw gets a jagged torn break.
- **Every piece is a sprite:** front, a cut edge tinted `#B49D78` offset ≈ 1 px, a sharp and a soft shadow (`rgb(62,40,22)`, blurred 2.5 and 9 px), and an optional pale back `#E9DFC8`. All are world-anchored; only the vignette is screen-space.
- **Sources.** The user's own scans first; otherwise engravings that are public domain on their own file page (Wikimedia Commons, Internet Archive books before 1931, Library of Congress items with a clear rights statement, U.S. government works), each logged with page and licence. People are cut-paper silhouettes, never a likeness.

## 4. Colour logic

- **The ground is old paper.** Cream table, warmer card for slips and cut margins, grey-white newsprint for clippings.
- **Three faded papers for structure.** Teal, ochre and mustard sheets carry composition and mood: teal as sky and cool distance, ochre as warmth and weight, mustard as sun, light and highlight. One leads per chapter.
- **Ink is near-black brown**, never pure black. Red ink is for marks of attention (tags, strike-throughs, the thing that is wrong); blue ink is the second plate and the calm label.
- **Colour sits on paper, not light:** no glows, no gradients but the vignette, no saturated digital colour, no pure white.
- **Never coloured:** the engraved lines (always ink), the table, the shadows (always warm brown).

## 5. Type and captions

- **Faces (SIL Open Font License):** IM FELL English (roman, italic and small caps) for titles, ransom letters and prices; Courier Prime regular and bold for every typed slip, tag and caption. IM FELL's old-style figures read as letters (1 as I, 0 as o), so any digit in a ransom headline is set in Courier Prime Bold.
- **Ransom letters.** Each letter is its own torn scrap; face, scrap paper (card, newsprint, mustard, ochre, teal or black) and ink (dark or card-white) cycle through eight combinations, size varying 86–114 %. One pastes on per eighth note.
- **Typed slips.** A blank slip slaps down, then characters appear one cell at a time, up to 55 per second (never longer than 1 s), ink weight varying 85–100 % like a worn ribbon.
- **Red strike.** A 7 px round-capped red ink stroke crosses a typed number in 0.25 s; the new figure then pastes in beside it.
- **Captions.** The typed slip in the spoken language only, one line per slip; hold ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Two slips may stack when a sentence must split.

## 6. Motion

- **Frame rate.** 24 fps for YouTube, 30 fps for a reel. Paper pieces step at 12 fps (`floor(t × 12) / 12`); the camera and shadows' softness are evaluated every frame.
- **The slap.** 0.17 s before landing, a piece is 12 % larger, lifted (height 4.5), offset 16 px left and 24 px up and turned 5° more; it drops with an ease-in, lands, then wobbles: 0.9° alternating left and right, decaying ×0.45 per step for 0.34 s, with a 2 px vertical bounce.
- **The slide.** Large papers enter from off-frame at height 2, travelling 0.4–0.6 s, and land with a back ease (overshoot 1.9) and a smaller wobble.
- **Jitter while moving.** Moving pieces shake up to ±0.7 px and ±0.25° per step (hashed from id and step); resting pieces are still.
- **Flight.** Flying pieces bob ±7 px and ±2° on a 0.8 s sine, leaving a dashed ink route (3 px, 14/12 dash).
- **Flips** squash the width to 4 % and show the pale back past the midpoint; a 180° spin takes 0.33 s with a slight overshoot.
- **Impact shake.** A heavy landing shakes the camera 8 px, decaying with exp(−9 s) over 0.5 s.
- **What never moves:** the table texture, the shadow direction (always down-right), the engraved lines inside a piece.

## 7. Camera

A 2D camera over the table: centre, zoom (interpolated in log space) and a slight roll; cubic in-out between keys.

| Move | What it expresses | What it can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Slow drift and push (×1.0 → ×1.14 over 4–5 s) | looking closer while things arrive | every arrangement; never fully still |
| Push into one detail (×1 → ×9.4 in 0.8 s, then creeping) | "this one, here" | the flaw, the signature, the date, the odd one out |
| Through the hole (ease-in to ×14 in 0.75 s, next scene clipped inside the opening) | going inside the object's story | chapter turns; frames, windows, keyholes |
| Whip with a paper seam (0.4 s, in-out, the next table 1.08 frame widths to the side) | a jump in time or place | "years later", another country, the other side |
| Pull back after a burst (×1.08 → ×0.62, ease-out) | scale, dispersal | copies scattering, how many, where they went |
| Rise along a growing bar | magnitude | prices, ratios, totals over time |

**Allowed transitions:** through a hole, the paper-seam whip, a sheet sliding over the whole frame, a hard cut on a slap. **Forbidden:** dissolves, blur transitions, 3D page curls, light leaks, film-burn effects.

## 9. Native moves

1. **The assembly.** Torn colour papers slide in 0.2–0.3 s apart, then the hero engraving lands on them with a slap and a harp run. Fits content like: an opening; "this is the object"; setting a period.
2. **Paste the word.** A ransom headline appears letter by letter on eighth notes, each scrap different. Fits content like: titles and chapter cards; a name; a demand or slogan.
3. **Type, strike, paste.** A typed sum appears on a slip, a red stroke strikes the number out, a bigger number pastes in beside it in ransom letters. Fits content like: price then vs now; a correction; a record broken.
4. **Cut it apart.** Engraved scissors travel along a line, rocking ±4° on each step; a thin slit follows; the pieces part 12–14 px, then burst outward, flipping as they fly. Fits content like: something divided or scattered; a break-up; one becoming many.
5. **Through the hole.** The camera dives into an opening in a cut-out (an oval frame, a window, a keyhole) and the next collage is already inside it. Fits content like: entering a story; looking inside a thing; chapter changes.
6. **The engraved tower.** A bar of swelling lines grows from the table, the camera rising with it, a horn at the top, a torn break where the scale snaps. Fits content like: price growth; compound numbers; comparisons too big to draw.
7. **Re-inked plates.** An archive print's colour plates lift apart and land again, one shifted or swapped. Fits content like: printing errors; forgeries; how something was made.
8. **Point and tag.** Card slips slap beside a detail, each with an inked leader ending in a dot. Fits content like: anatomy of an object; "notice this"; labelling parts.

## 10. Build

Follow [building](../../../references/building.md). For this style:

- Bake the paper pieces offline (Python with NumPy, SciPy and Pillow, fixed seeds) as PNGs at 1.5× world scale, with a manifest of sizes, crops and text metrics.
- Draw the film on one canvas from `t`: each sprite as shadow (sharp and soft mixed by height), cut edge, then front or back; one keyframe pose function (time, x, y, rotation, height, x-scale, ease, landing wobble) adds the settle and the step jitter.
- Step paper with `floor(t × 12) / 12` but evaluate the camera at `t`; seed every jitter with a hash of the piece id and step.
- Transitions are clipping paths: the next scene drawn inside the hole's shape in screen space; the whip draws both scenes side by side with a clip at the seam.
- Typed slips are a blank sprite plus the text sprite clipped to `start + n × advance`, using Courier Prime's monospaced advance.
- Engraved data comes from one function: a tone field and a line coordinate in, swelling-line ink out, with 1 px of anti-aliasing.

## 11. Pitfalls

- **Clean digital edges.** A cut-out without its rough white margin, or a shadow that doesn't change with height, reads as a slideshow.
- **Everything on twos, including the camera.** Then the whole frame stutters; only paper steps.
- **Scans with backgrounds.** A grey rectangle of page behind an engraving kills the cut-out; mask, then dilate the margin.
- **Old-style figures.** Digits in IM FELL look like letters; use Courier Prime for numbers.
- **Rights.** "No known restrictions" is not public domain. Check each file page; use silhouettes for real people.
- **Upscaled engravings** turn to mush past about 1.5× their scan.

## 12. Variation space

The agent decides per video: the hero engravings, which colour paper leads each chapter, the march's key and tempo (108–126 BPM), the tag wording, the number of chapters, how much the table drifts.

- **Three structures:** the object's life in stations (made → used → forgotten → rediscovered), one table per station; a price story told by typed sums, red strikes and an engraved tower; an idea's family tree, each chapter pasting a new branch of engravings onto one growing sheet.
- **Three openings:** the assembly of torn papers and the hero landing; the ransom headline pasting itself over an empty table and the hero sliding under it; scissors already cutting through an engraving on frame 1.
- **Three endings:** a slow pull back over the final arrangement as a music box plays; a dive through a last opening onto a fresh table that echoes the first frame; the pieces sliding off one by one until only the hero remains.
